Strategic Framework for Debt Collection and Asset Recovery in the Context of Will Financeira S.A. Extrajudicial Liquidation
1. Executive Summary
The Brazilian financial ecosystem has recently witnessed a systemic disruption with the forced extrajudicial liquidation of Will Financeira S.A. Crédito, Financiamento e Investimento, operating commercially as Will Bank. Decreed by the Central Bank of Brazil (BCB) on January 21, 2026, under Presidential Act No. 1.376, this regulatory intervention presents a highly complex, multi-dimensional challenge for asset recovery and consumer debt collection. Will Bank was not a traditional institution; it was a rapidly scaling digital fintech fundamentally embedded in the financial lives of over six million predominantly low-income consumers, particularly concentrated in the Brazilian Northeast. The objective of this comprehensive investigation is to deliver an exhaustive, expert-level operational and strategic knowledge base. This framework is designed to optimize the collection of the remaining active credit portfolio held by the liquidated estate, overseen by the appointed liquidator, EFB Regimes Especiais de Empresas Ltda, under the technical responsibility of Eduardo Felix Bianchini.

The core challenge for the liquidating estate lies at the intersection of severe operational friction, unprecedented reputational collapse, and profound debtor socioeconomic vulnerability. Will Bank actively built its credit portfolio by targeting the unbanked and lower-income strata, proudly positioning itself as the bank that says "yes" to the invisible populations of the credit market. Consequently, the sudden cessation of services has locked millions of economically fragile customers out of their primary financial hub. Standard modern payment rails, such as PIX and automatic application-based debits, have been suspended, forcing all debt repayments into a manual, friction-heavy boleto (bank slip) generation process. This structural disruption is compounded by severe reputational damage stemming from the broader collapse and ongoing multibillion-real fraud investigations surrounding Will Bank's parent company, the Banco Master Conglomerate.

This report synthesizes public regulatory records, Central Bank documentation, Brazilian legal frameworks governing bankruptcies (Lei 6.024/1974), and the latest academic literature on behavioral economics and algorithmic nudging. It formulates distinct debtor hypotheses, recognizing that the current active portfolio contains both technical defaults—caused entirely by payment friction and platform collapse—and opportunistic defaults, which are justified by moral hazard and deep institutional mistrust. To maximize the recovery of the mass of assets, the traditional, aggressive debt collection paradigm must be entirely replaced by a consultative, empathetically driven framework. The strategies outlined herein advocate leveraging empirically validated behavioral nudges, such as percentage-based savings framing, social norms, and loss aversion, while rigorously adhering to the Brazilian Consumer Defense Code (CDC) and the General Data Protection Law (LGPD). Ultimately, this framework provides a segmentation strategy that isolates willingness to pay from capacity to pay, deploying specialized collector profiles trained in Nonviolent Communication (NVC) to navigate the profound anxieties of a distressed, underbanked population.

2. Timeline of Will Bank’s Relevant History and Liquidation Context
Understanding the historical trajectory of Will Bank is vital for mapping the psychological state, financial habits, and risk profile of its debtor base. The institution's rapid growth, aggressive marketing strategies, and subsequent integration into a highly scrutinized financial conglomerate directly inform the complexities of the current collection environment.

2.1 Origins, Hyper-Growth, and Financial Inclusion (2017–2023)
The institution was initially founded in May 2017 under the brand "Meu Pag!" by entrepreneurs Giovanni Piana, Walter Piana, and Felipe Felix, designed specifically to capture the heavily underserved, lower-income credit market in Brazil. Recognizing the limitations of traditional banking in reaching the interior of the country, the fintech rebranded as Will Bank in 2019, launching a full suite of digital banking services. The brand leaned heavily into a positioning that resonated with the working class, particularly in the Northeast region, which eventually concentrated a staggering 60% of its customer base. Will Bank provided over 1.1 million citizens with their very first credit cards, effectively acting as an engine for financial inclusion but simultaneously taking on significant credit risk profiles.

By utilizing pop-culture figures, influencers, and aggressive digital marketing, Will Bank scaled its operations at a hyper-growth pace. By the end of 2020, the institution reported revenues exceeding R$ 500 million. In 2021, a major capital injection of R$ 250 million from prominent investment firms XP and Atmos Capital pushed revenues beyond R$ 800 million. By the end of 2022, Will Financeira reported R$ 3.7 billion in total assets, a net equity of R$ 345 million, and an active credit portfolio of R$ 1.2 billion, handling nearly R$ 13 billion in credit card transactions. In 2023, the institution expanded its product offerings to include personal loans through the "Emprestai" product and FGTS birthday withdrawal anticipations. However, the aggressive issuance of credit to individuals with low credit scores and low historical banking penetration inherently carried massive systemic risk, manifesting as exceptionally high default rates typical of this vulnerable market segment.

2.2 The Banco Master Acquisition and Systemic Vulnerability (2024–2025)
In February 2024, the Master Conglomerate (Banco Master), led by Daniel Vorcaro, acquired Will Bank in an effort to establish a robust digital ecosystem and distribution capillarity across the country. By the time of the acquisition, Will Bank had grown to service approximately 6 million customers with a workforce of 1,200 employees. While the acquisition appeared to be a strategic expansion, the parent conglomerate was masking deep systemic vulnerabilities and engaging in highly aggressive capital operations.

Late 2025 saw the unravelling of the Master Conglomerate amid intense regulatory scrutiny and investigations into massive financial fraud. Investigations suggested that a scheme had been orchestrated to cover up an astonishing financial shortfall of approximately R$ 52 billion within Banco Master, allegedly involving connections with influential political figures to stave off regulatory intervention. Consequently, on November 18, 2025, the Central Bank of Brazil imposed a Special Temporary Administration Regime (RAET) on Banco Master Múltiplo S/A. The Central Bank justified this intervention as an attempt to preserve the functioning and operational integrity of its heavily utilized subsidiary, Will Financeira.

2.3 Systemic Collapse, Payment Friction, and Liquidation (January 2026)
The regulatory attempt to ring-fence Will Financeira from the contagion of its parent conglomerate ultimately failed. The decisive operational trigger occurred on January 19, 2026, when Will Financeira failed to honor its payment settlement schedule with the Mastercard network. This critical failure resulted in the immediate blockage of Will Bank's participation in the payment arrangement, rendering its credit and debit cards instantly useless across the nation. With liquidity completely drained and systemic insolvency undeniable, the Central Bank of Brazil enacted Presidential Act No. 1.376 on January 21, 2026, formally decreeing the extrajudicial liquidation of Will Financeira S.A..

The immediate aftermath was catastrophic for the consumer base. Millions of customers awoke to frozen accounts, disabled credit cards, and completely blocked PIX functionalities, sparking nationwide panic and outrage. The Credit Guarantee Fund (FGC) intervened rapidly, implementing a novel system to anticipate payments up to R$ 1,000 directly through the disabled Will Bank application to alleviate immediate hardship for the poorest consumers, while larger sums were relegated to the standard FGC bureaucratic queue. Crucially, for the asset recovery operation, the liquidation dictated that all outstanding debtor invoices (faturas) could no longer be paid via modern digital rails. PIX and automatic debit were suspended, and debtors were restricted to a manual boleto generation process. This regression in payment technology introduced massive friction into the repayment cycle, directly contributing to technical defaults.

4. Macroeconomic and Reputational Landscape
To design an effective debt recovery strategy, the liquidator cannot view the Will Bank portfolio in a vacuum. The collection effort is taking place against the backdrop of a severely strained Brazilian macroeconomic environment and an unprecedented reputational crisis specific to the institution.

The macroeconomic reality for the Brazilian working class is currently defined by extreme financial pressure. Recent market intelligence indicates that household debt is growing rapidly across Latin America, with delinquency rates reaching alarming levels due to sustained high interest rates (Selic) and inflation. Brazil faces one of the most severe consumer debt crises in the region, with approximately 77.5% of families in debt and nearly 30% projected to default in the near term. Will Bank's portfolio is overwhelmingly concentrated in this exact demographic: individuals earning between one and two minimum wages, residing in the Northeast or interior municipalities, who rely on credit cards not for discretionary spending, but as an essential extension of their income to purchase basic goods like food and medicine. For these consumers, the sudden cancellation of their Will Bank credit card is not merely an inconvenience; it represents an immediate and catastrophic rupture of their household cash flow.

Compounding this macroeconomic strain is the severe reputational damage inflicted by the collapse of the Banco Master Conglomerate. The media landscape is saturated with reports of the R$ 52 billion fraud investigation involving Daniel Vorcaro and the broader conglomerate. This public narrative creates a highly toxic collection environment. Consumers are inundated with news of corruption and elite financial malfeasance, which directly fosters moral hazard among the debtor base. A significant portion of the public falsely conflates the criminal investigations of the bank's owners with the dissolution of their own consumer contracts. The perception is that the institution is "thieving" or "corrupt," providing a psychological justification for debtors to withhold payment. Consequently, any collection strategy must proactively dismantle this narrative, reframing the payment not as enriching a corrupt bank, but as a necessary legal step to protect the consumer's own future financial citizenship.

This persona represents the core of Will Bank's historical growth strategy.

Demographic Profile: C/D/E socioeconomic class, predominantly residing in the Northeast region or smaller interior municipalities. Monthly household income typically hovers around one to two minimum wages.

Financial Behavior: This user relied heavily on the Will Bank credit card to manage basic survival logistics. Up to 70% of their transactions were for essential goods at supermarkets and pharmacies. Current State & Pain Points: They are experiencing acute, immediate financial shock. The sudden cancellation of the card has broken their month-to-month cash flow. Collection Implication: Traditional, aggressive collection will fail categorically. This persona literally cannot pay the full balance, regardless of the threats applied. This persona highlights the operational consequences of the liquidation.

Demographic Profile: Younger demographic, heavily reliant on smartphone banking, and entirely accustomed to seamless UI/UX environments.

6. Collector Profile Recommendations
The traditional archetype of a Brazilian debt collector—authoritative, high-pressure, legally threatening, and relentless—is fundamentally counterproductive in the specific context of a liquidated fintech serving vulnerable populations. Academic research indicates that in contexts of financial scarcity, aggressive tactics trigger cognitive tunneling, defensive anger, and avoidance behaviors, entirely shutting down the possibility of negotiation. The liquidator must deploy a modernized "Collector Profile" fundamentally based on behavioral psychology and empathetic mediation.

6.1 Core Competencies and Psychological Traits
High Empathy and Active Listening: The collector must view their role as a financial mediator rather than an enforcer. They will routinely encounter debtors who are angry, confused, and feel betrayed by the sudden loss of their credit line. The ability to de-escalate tension and validate the debtor's frustration regarding the bank's collapse without admitting institutional liability is critical.

Proficiency in Nonviolent Communication (NVC): Grounded in Marshall Rosenberg's framework, collectors must be trained to separate objective observation from subjective evaluation. They must identify the underlying needs of the debtor (e.g., fear of hunger, need for security, confusion over boletos) and formulate requests that feel collaborative rather than coercive.

Algorithmic and Technological Literacy: Modern debt collection relies heavily on CRM software, AI voice agents, and omnichannel platforms. The modern collector must comfortably navigate these interfaces, understanding when to let an automated nudge do the work and when nuanced human intervention is necessary to close a complex restructuring deal.

Regulatory Fluency and Compliance: Collectors must be thoroughly trained on the strict boundaries established by the Consumer Defense Code (CDC) and the LGPD. They must understand that any communication that can be construed as harassing, publicly shaming, or violating data privacy will immediately expose the liquidating estate to costly moral damage lawsuits.

6.2 Specialized Training Regimen
The human collection force should undergo specialized onboarding specifically tailored to the unique Will Bank scenario. This training must include intensive role-playing exercises addressing the most common and difficult objection they will face: "Why should I pay a bankrupt, corrupt bank?" Collectors must be equipped with standardized, legally vetted, and empathetic rebuttals that emphasize the preservation of the debtor's credit score (Serasa/SPC) and clearly explain the legal mandate of the Central Bank's appointed liquidator.

7. Scientific Literature Synthesis: Applying Behavioral Economics
A robust, modern debt recovery strategy cannot rely on intuition; it must be underpinned by empirical science. Exhaustive research in behavioral public policy, algorithmic choice architecture, and financial psychology provides critical, actionable insights into how the Will Bank debtor base will react to various collection efforts.

7.1 Nudging and Choice Architecture in Debt Recovery
Cass Sunstein and Richard Thaler’s Nobel-winning concept of "Nudging" involves altering the choice architecture—the way options are presented—to predictably change behavior without forbidding options or significantly changing economic incentives. In the context of debt collection, a massive 13-million-person field experiment analyzed by the NBER and PNAS demonstrated that minor, behaviorally informed changes in communication yield statistically massive results in repayment rates.

Percentage Framing vs. Absolute Dollar Framing: The study found that presenting settlement offers as a percentage (e.g., "Save 40% on your outstanding debt") is significantly more effective than absolute dollar framing (e.g., "Save R$ 400"). This is particularly potent for low-income populations, who perceive percentage discounts as subjectively larger and more valuable, magnifying the perceived benefit of the settlement.

The "Two-Step" Action Plan: Providing exactly two clear, sequential action steps in an email or SMS (e.g., "1. Click here to view your offer. 2. Copy the barcode to your bank app") significantly reduces cognitive overload. The research proved this is superior to both a single vague instruction and a complex, multi-step bureaucratic process.

7.2 Social Norms and Peer Comparison
Behavioral science consistently indicates that individuals are highly influenced by the perceived behavior of their peers, a concept known as social norming. A study by the University of Warwick focused on loan defaults demonstrated that messaging based on social norms—for example, sending an SMS stating, "Nine out of ten people in your city have already renegotiated their pending balances"—resulted in a nearly 7% higher probability of payment from delinquent borrowers. In the specific context of Will Bank, where the prevailing public sentiment and social media narrative assumes everyone is defaulting due to the bankruptcy, countering this toxic narrative with positive social proof is absolutely vital to altering debtor behavior.

7.3 Financial Scarcity and the "Call First, Pay Later" Paradigm
Research published by Cambridge University on debt collection in the context of severe financial scarcity highlights a critical phenomenon: demanding immediate, full repayment from severely distressed individuals triggers a psychological "flight or freeze" response. Debtors simply ignore letters and block numbers because the financial demand wildly exceeds their capacity, causing acute psychological pain. The study proves that nudges focusing purely on establishing contact (e.g., "Please contact us so we can understand your current situation") rather than demanding payment dramatically increase engagement rates and lead to a much higher volume of eventual agreements to sustainable repayment plans.

7.4 Nonviolent Communication (NVC) and Empathic Scripting
When applied to collection scripts, NVC shifts the language from accusatory to collaborative. Instead of the traditional, threatening approach ("You have failed to pay your invoice and will be reported"), the framing becomes fundamentally empathetic: "We noticed a pending balance on your account. We completely understand that the recent sudden changes to the bank may have caused confusion and disrupted your routine. How can we help you resolve this today?" This approach radically reduces debtor defensiveness and builds the necessary trust required for effective asset recovery.

9. Recommended Collection Strategies by Debtor Segment

9.1 Strategy for Q1 (High Capacity / High Willingness)
The "Frictionless Nudge": For this segment, the sole barrier to repayment is the lack of the functional app and the inability to use PIX. The strategy must focus on frictionless delivery. Send highly personalized WhatsApp messages and SMS containing the exact barcode line (linha digitável) for the boleto, requiring only a simple "copy and paste" action by the user.

Security Assurance Protocol: Because of rampant fraud and phishing post-liquidation, these users are terrified of paying a fake boleto. Communications must include verifiable, localized data (e.g., the last four digits of their CPF and the exact purchase amount of a recent historical transaction) to prove authenticity. 9.2 Strategy for Q2 (Low Capacity / High Willingness)
The "Lifeline" Restructuring: Acknowledge the trauma of the loss of their revolving credit line. Offer immediate, pre-approved installment plans (parcelamento) directly via a frictionless self-service digital portal.

Micro-Payments and Habituation: Break down the total debt into micro-installments that comfortably fit within a minimum wage budget, applying the "Call First, Pay Later" academic logic. The primary goal is to keep the debtor engaged and paying something, establishing a habit of repayment, rather than demanding a lump sum they mathematically do not possess.

9.3 Strategy for Q3 (High Capacity / Low Willingness)
The "Reputational Nudge": This segment believes they can outwait the liquidator or that the debt is legally void. Communications must heavily utilize the psychological principle of loss aversion. Emphasize exactly what they stand to lose: their credit score, their ability to secure loans elsewhere, and their broader financial citizenship.

Strategic Escalation: Initiate with soft educational reminders regarding the role of the liquidator, rapidly escalating to formal notifications of impending credit bureau reporting (Serasa/SPC). The language should pivot from empathetic to highly formal and legally precise, severing the conceptual link between Banco Master's alleged fraud and their personal, legally binding consumer contract.

9.4 Strategy for Q4 (Low Capacity / Low Willingness)
Algorithmic Containment: Do not waste expensive human collector hours or high-cost telephony on this segment. Utilize fully automated AI voice agents and periodic SMS to deliver highly discounted, aggressive settlement offers (e.g., 70-80% off the principal balance).

Secondary Market Preparation: Acknowledge that the liquidator will not recover these funds directly. Package this heavily distressed, non-performing portfolio for eventual sale to specialized "crédito podre" (distressed credit) funds (e.g., Jive Investments) at a steep discount, cleaning the liquidator's balance sheet and generating immediate, albeit reduced, liquidity for the estate.

11. Legal, Ethical, and Reputational Constraints
Operating a debt collection strategy within the confines of an extrajudicial liquidation requires meticulously navigating a minefield of Brazilian regulatory statutes, civil law frameworks, and highest-court jurisprudence.

11.1 Lei 6.024/1974 and Superior Court Jurisprudence on Interest
A highly critical legal nuance must be fully understood regarding the application of Lei 6.024/74, the specific law governing the extrajudicial liquidation of financial institutions. Article 18 of this law dictates that the accrual of interest, penalties, and monetary correction is suspended upon the decree of liquidation. However, established jurisprudence from the Superior Court of Justice (STJ) explicitly clarifies that this suspension applies strictly to the liabilities of the bank (i.e., the money the bankrupt estate owes to its creditors), ensuring the equal treatment of creditors (par conditio creditorum).

This legal suspension does not forgive or freeze the interest or monetary correction owed by the debtors of the bank. Consumers who took credit from Will Bank are still legally bound to the original terms, interest rates, and penalties of their contracts. Nevertheless, from a strategic, behavioral, and ethical standpoint, brutally enforcing high revolving credit interest rates on a highly vulnerable population that has just lost access to their banking platform is guaranteed to trigger mass litigation, severe public backlash, and overwhelming default. The liquidator should utilize their legal prerogative to proactively freeze or heavily discount interest accrual for cooperative debtors, using this concession as a powerful psychological negotiation tool to accelerate the recovery of the core principal asset.

11.2 Consumer Defense Code (CDC) and the Lei do Superendividamento
The Brazilian Consumer Defense Code (CDC) strictly prohibits abusive collection practices (Article 42), making any form of public shaming, excessive harassment, or coercion entirely illegal and subject to immediate moral damage claims. Furthermore, the recent "Lei do Superendividamento" (Law 14.181/2021) places a strict legal obligation on creditors to foster responsible renegotiation practices that explicitly preserve the "existential minimum" of the debtor. Given the extremely low-income nature of Will Bank's core demographic, ignoring this specific law will render collection efforts legally void and expose the liquidating estate to massive counter-lawsuits. Every collection script and settlement algorithm must be vetted against these standards.

11.3 LGPD and Data Integrity
With the bank's digital infrastructure compromised and operational teams likely in disarray, maintaining the strict integrity of the debtor database is paramount. Any transfer of customer data to third-party collection agencies (Assessorias de Cobrança) or data enrichment bureaus must be rigorously audited to ensure absolute compliance with the General Data Protection Law (LGPD). A data leak in this highly sensitive transition period would invite severe regulatory sanctions from the National Data Protection Authority (ANPD) and completely destroy any remaining shred of consumer trust.

11.4 Reputational Risk Mitigation for the Regulator
While Will Bank's brand reputation is already deeply and perhaps irreparably compromised by the Banco Master collapse , the collection strategy must aggressively protect the reputation of the liquidator and, by extension, the Central Bank of Brazil. Communications must be hyper-transparent, clearly identifying that the collection is an official, legally mandated procedure executed by an independent agent to recover assets for the benefit of all legitimate creditors. The messaging must strictly and constantly distance the current recovery operation from the previous fraudulent management of the institution.
